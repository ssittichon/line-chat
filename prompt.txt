| Element | Business meaning | Examples | Main statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assets | Resource/right that the business has or controls | Cash, AR, Inventory, Equipment | Statement of Financial Position |
| Liabilities | Obligation of the business | AP, Loan, Accrued Expense | Statement of Financial Position |
| Equity | Residual interest after liabilities | Capital, Retained Earnings | Statement of Financial Position |
| Revenue / Income | Activity of the period that increases performance | Sales Revenue, Service Revenue | Profit or Loss |
| Expenses | Resources used/consumed to create period performance | Salary, Rent, COGS, Depreciation | Profit or Loss |

## 6. Accounting Equation


**Accounting Equation**

`Assets = Liabilities + Equity`

- Assets = Resources / Rights
- Liabilities = Creditor claims
- Equity = Owner's residual interest

## 8. Normal Balance and Debit / Credit

|---|---|---|---|

### Adjustment concepts

- **Reconciliation:** compare two related data sources and verify that they agree

**Definition:** Chart of Accounts (COA) is the structure of account codes and account names used by an organization to classify, record, and summarize accounting information in ERP and GL.

# PART VIII — Key Rules for Non-Finance Users

1. Start with **what happened in the business**, not with Dr/Cr.
2. Determine the **accounts affected**; double-entry generally means at least two accounts.
3. Identify each account's **Accounting Element** and whether it increases or decreases.
4. Apply **Normal Balance** to decide Debit/Credit.
5. Do not equate **cash movement** with Revenue or Expense.
6. Treat a **document as evidence**, not automatic proof that a GL entry exists.
7. A created transaction may still fail to post, and a posted transaction may still be mapped incorrectly.
8. Use **COA + Account Mapping** to connect ERP transactions to the correct GL accounts.
9. Reconcile **Sub-ledger and Control Account** before relying on financial reporting.
10. If a number looks unusual, **trace backward one layer at a time**.
11. Use a common accounting backbone across business models, but let **economic substance** drive detailed accounting application.
